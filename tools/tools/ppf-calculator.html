About the PPF Calculator

Public Provident Fund is the one investment where you can see your exact maturity amount in advance and not worry about it for 15 years. Put ₹1.5 lakh a year into PPF at 7.1% and you'll walk away with about ₹40.68 lakh after 15 years. You contributed ₹22.5 lakh, the government paid you ₹18.18 lakh in interest, and not a single rupee of it is taxable. Try finding that combination anywhere else.

PPF was launched in 1968 and has been reset every quarter since 2016. The rate moves with the 10-year government bond yield — when bond yields rise, PPF rises, and vice versa. In FY 2023-24 it sat at 7.1%, unchanged for the last several quarters because bond yields have stayed in a narrow band. Post Office Time Deposits currently pay 6.9% for 5 years, and 5-year bank FDs hover at 7.0–7.5%. PPF's rate is competitive, but its real edge is the tax treatment.

Here's the tax math. Suppose you're in the 30% slab. A 5-year bank FD at 7.5% effectively pays you 5.25% after tax. A PPF at 7.1% pays you 7.1% — no tax, no TDS, no reporting. On ₹22.5 lakh over 15 years, that difference compounds to roughly ₹4–5 lakh of extra post-tax wealth. Add the 80C deduction on your contributions (up to ₹1.5 lakh per year under old regime) and PPF becomes the highest after-tax fixed-income product most Indians can access legally.

The catch is liquidity. You can't touch PPF for 15 years except for a partial withdrawal from year 7 (50% of the balance at the end of year 4). Loans against PPF are allowed between years 3 and 6, capped at 25% of the balance at the end of the second year. After maturity, you can extend in 5-year blocks — with fresh deposits (extension with contribution) or without (extension without contribution), where the corpus keeps earning interest without further additions.

For Americans and Europeans, PPF has no direct equivalent — it's an India-specific instrument available only to Indian residents and a few categories of non-resident Indians. NRIs who opened PPF before becoming NRI can hold to maturity, but new accounts aren't allowed. The closest parallels are US Series I bonds (currently 4.28% as of late 2024, exempt from state tax) or a Roth IRA where you're already past the income limit for direct contributions.

One practical tip: deposit before the 5th of April each year. PPF interest is calculated on the minimum balance between the 5th and the last day of each month. Deposit ₹1.5 lakh on April 4th and it earns interest for all 12 months of that financial year. Deposit the same amount on April 10th and you lose a full month's interest — roughly ₹890 on ₹1.5 lakh at 7.1%, every single year. Over 15 years, that timing gap costs about ₹18,000.

Whether PPF belongs in your portfolio depends on your horizon and tax bracket. If you're in the 5% or 0% slab, a 7.5% FD often wins after tax, and it's far more flexible. If you're in the 20% or 30% slab and can lock money for 15 years, PPF is hard to beat for the debt portion of your allocation. Most financial planners suggest treating PPF as the "safe bucket" and letting equity mutual funds handle growth — not choosing between the two.

How to Use

  1. 1

    Set currency (default INR). PPF is an Indian instrument, so INR is selected by default. Switch if you want to model the numbers in another currency for comparison — though PPF itself can only be opened in INR.

  2. 2

    Enter your yearly deposit. The slider maxes out at ₹1.5 lakh because that's the PPF ceiling per financial year. You can type a lower amount — the minimum is ₹500 per year. Most people contribute the full ₹1.5 lakh to maximize the 80C deduction.

  3. 3

    Check the current rate. The government announces PPF rates every quarter. As of late 2024 it's 7.1%. If you're modeling a future scenario, 7.0–7.2% is a reasonable long-term assumption based on the last 8 years of data.

  4. 4

    Pick your tenure. PPF matures at 15 years, but you can extend to 20, 25, and so on. The slider goes up to 50 years so you can see what a multigenerational PPF would grow into — though in practice, most people withdraw at 15 and reinvest elsewhere.

  5. 5

    Read the tax badge. The result panel shows a green "Fully tax-free" note confirming EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) status. Copy the result to compare against FD or NPS numbers on a spreadsheet.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is PPF and who can open one?
PPF is a long-term savings scheme backed by the Government of India, with a 15-year lock-in and a fixed rate revised quarterly. Any Indian resident can open one — salaried, self-employed, minors through a guardian. You can also open a second PPF account in a minor child's name, but the combined yearly limit across all accounts is still ₹1.5 lakh. NRIs can't open a new PPF account, though accounts opened before becoming an NRI continue until maturity. You can open PPF at any nationalized bank (SBI, PNB), major private banks (HDFC, ICICI), or the post office — the rate and rules are identical everywhere.
How is PPF interest calculated?
PPF compounds yearly, but the trick is in the monthly rule. Interest each month is calculated on the lowest balance between the 5th and the last day of that month. So if you deposit ₹1.5 lakh on April 4th, it earns interest for 12 months of the year. Deposit it on April 10th and you lose April's interest entirely — that's the ₹890 mistake. Interest is credited to your account on March 31st each year. The formula is: Year-end balance = (Opening balance + Deposits during year) × (1 + rate). This repeats every year for 15 years.
Is PPF interest taxable?
No. PPF enjoys EEE status — Exempt on contribution (80C deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh), Exempt on interest accrued, Exempt on maturity amount. This is why PPF is a rare "triple tax-free" instrument. In the old tax regime, contributing ₹1.5 lakh to PPF saves you ₹46,800 in tax if you're in the 30% slab (plus cess). In the new regime (from FY 2023-24), the 80C deduction isn't available, but the interest and maturity remain fully tax-free — so PPF is still worth it if you want tax-free growth.
Can I withdraw money from PPF before 15 years?
Partially, from year 7. You can withdraw up to 50% of the balance at the end of year 4, once per year. Say you deposited ₹1.5 lakh yearly for 6 years and the balance at end of year 4 was ₹6.7 lakh — you can withdraw up to ₹3.35 lakh starting from year 7. Between years 3 and 6 you can take a loan against your PPF balance at 2% above the PPF rate, capped at 25% of the year-2 closing balance. Full withdrawal is only allowed at maturity (15 years) or in specific cases like death of the account holder.
What happens at PPF maturity after 15 years?
You have three choices. First, withdraw the full amount — tax-free. Second, extend for 5 years with fresh contributions (you keep depositing ₹1.5 lakh/year and keep earning 7.1% tax-free). Third, extend for 5 years without fresh contributions — the existing corpus keeps compounding at the PPF rate but you don't add money. Option three is popular with retirees. If you do nothing, the account continues to earn interest but doesn't get fresh contributions until you formally submit the extension form at your bank. So if you want to keep PPF alive after 15 years, submit Form H within one year of maturity.
Is PPF better than FD or mutual funds?
Depends on your tax slab and horizon. Against a 5-year bank FD at 7.5%, if you're in the 30% slab, the FD effectively gives 5.25% after tax — PPF at 7.1% tax-free clearly wins. If you're in the 0–5% slab, the FD is competitive and far more liquid. Against equity mutual funds, a SIP in an index fund has historically returned 11–12% over 15-year windows but with drawdowns. PPF gives certainty. Most planners suggest putting your debt allocation into PPF and growth allocation into equity funds — not choosing one over the other. Never compare PPF to small-cap funds; they're playing different games.
What is the minimum and maximum I can deposit in PPF?
Minimum ₹500 per financial year — miss this and the account becomes dormant, costing you ₹50 to reactivate. Maximum ₹1.5 lakh per financial year, across all PPF accounts you hold (self + minor child combined). You can deposit in one lump sum or in up to 12 monthly installments. Many people set up a ₹12,500 monthly auto-debit to hit the ₹1.5 lakh cap without thinking about it. Deposits above ₹1.5 lakh don't earn interest and are returned without any benefit — banks simply reject them at the time of deposit.
