About the NPS Calculator

Start NPS at 30 with ₹10,000 a month and you retire at 60 with roughly ₹2.26 crore. You'll have put in ₹36 lakh yourself, the market will have added ₹1.90 crore on top, and you'll walk away with a ₹1.35 crore tax-free lump sum plus a ₹45,000 monthly pension for life. That math only works if you start early — begin at 45 instead and the same ₹10,000 a month gets you about ₹34 lakh total, not ₹2.26 crore. Fifteen years less compounding costs you ₹1.9 crore.

The National Pension System was launched in 2004 for government employees and opened to all citizens in 2009. The regulator is PFRDA. Unlike EPF or PPF, NPS is market-linked — your money sits in equity, corporate bonds, and government securities based on the allocation you choose (Active or Auto). The default Auto Choice (LC50) starts at 50% equity, drops 2.5% every year after 35, and lands at 10% equity by age 55. If you're 30 and comfortable with volatility, Active Choice with 75% equity (the Tier-I cap) has historically delivered 11–12% CAGR over 15-year windows.

The tax treatment is EET — Exempt on contribution (both employee and employer contributions deductible under Section 80CCD), Exempt on growth, Taxed on withdrawal of pension income. Your own contribution qualifies for an extra ₹50,000 deduction under 80CCD(1B) on top of the ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit, which makes NPS one of the few ways to save ₹2 lakh a year in deductible contributions. The lump-sum 60% at retirement is fully tax-free. Only the 40% forced into an annuity generates taxable pension — and there's no way to skip this 40% requirement anymore, though the government has hinted at revisiting it.

The annuity is where most people get disappointed. Park ₹90 lakh into an annuity at retirement and the insurer gives you 5.5–6.5% for life — that's ₹45,000–50,000 a month on ₹90 lakh. A simple 8% SWP (systematic withdrawal plan) from a debt fund would give you ₹60,000 a month with some capital preservation, but it isn't guaranteed for life. For retirees who value certainty and don't want to outlive their money, the annuity trade-off makes sense. For those with other income sources, choosing the minimum 40% annuity and keeping the rest in growth assets is usually smarter.

Exit rules matter. You can withdraw 25% of your contributions after 10 years for specific reasons — buying a house, medical treatment, higher education, marriage of children. Partial withdrawals are allowed three times total. Before 60, you can only exit if you've been in NPS for 10+ years, and then 80% must go into an annuity (20% tax-free lump sum). If you exit before 10 years, only your own contribution comes back and the returns go to the pension fund. So NPS isn't a product to enter and exit casually — it's a 25–30 year commitment.

For most salaried Indians, the sweet spot is contributing ₹10,000–15,000 a month through the corporate NPS route, where your employer's 10% of basic salary (up to ₹7.5 lakh/year under 80CCD(2)) is tax-free, and it doesn't eat into your 80C limit. That's the biggest NPS advantage most people miss. If you're self-employed, the ₹50,000 extra deduction under 80CCD(1B) makes NPS the single best tax-saving instrument after EPF. For low-income earners with no tax liability, NPS gives no tax benefit — better to stick with PPF.

Compare this tool's result against a simple SIP calculator. A ₹10,000 SIP for 30 years at 12% gives ₹3.5 crore — more than NPS because there's no annuity drag. But the SIP corpus isn't tax-free, and you have to manage withdrawal discipline yourself. NPS forces discipline, gives the ₹50,000 deduction, and hands you a guaranteed pension — for a modest return cost. Neither is universally better.

How to Use

  1. 1

    Enter your monthly contribution. This is what you plus your employer put into Tier-I each month. If your employer contributes 10% of basic, add that to your own number for a realistic total.

  2. 2

    Set your current age. The calculator automatically projects to age 60 — that's the mandatory NPS exit age for the corpus calculation. Younger entry = dramatically larger corpus.

  3. 3

    Pick your expected return. 10% is a reasonable middle assumption for a mixed allocation. Use 12% if you're in 75% equity Active Choice, 8–9% if you're conservative or approaching 55 (Auto Choice reduces equity sharply).

  4. 4

    Choose your annuity allocation. The PFRDA minimum is 40%. Anything above 40% reduces your tax-free lump sum but increases guaranteed lifetime pension. Test both 40% and 60% to see the trade-off.

  5. 5

    Read the four outputs. Corpus is the total at 60. Lump Sum is the 60% you get tax-free. Annuity Corpus goes to the insurer. Monthly Pension is what the insurer pays you for life. Copy the summary to compare NPS against PPF, EPF, or SIP numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NPS and who should invest in it?
NPS is a government-regulated, market-linked retirement scheme managed by PFRDA. It's best suited for salaried individuals aged 25–40 who are in the 20% or 30% tax bracket and want an extra ₹50,000 deduction under Section 80CCD(1B). The corporate NPS route (where your employer contributes 10% of basic) is especially attractive because employer contributions up to ₹7.5 lakh a year are fully tax-free and don't consume your 80C limit. Self-employed professionals with high tax liability also benefit. Low-income earners with no tax liability get little from NPS — PPF is better for them.
How is the NPS corpus calculated?
The corpus uses a monthly SIP formula: FV = P × [((1 + r/12)^n − 1) / (r/12)] × (1 + r/12), where P is your monthly contribution, r is the annual return, and n is the number of months from your current age to 60. Example: ₹10,000/month at 10% from age 30 to 60 = ₹2.26 crore. The NPS fund manager (SBI, HDFC, UTI, ICICI, etc.) actually computes NAV-based returns, not a fixed rate — this calculator uses a CAGR assumption so you can plan.
How is NPS taxed at retirement?
NPS Tier-I follows EET: contributions are tax-deductible (80CCD + extra ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B)), growth is tax-free, and the pension income is taxed as per slab. At 60, you take 60% of the corpus as a tax-free lump sum. The remaining 40% must be used to buy an annuity — the monthly pension from that annuity is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. If your total income at retirement is under ₹5 lakh, you may owe no tax on the pension due to rebate u/s 87A. Corpus above ₹5 lakh at exit also triggers 20% must-annuitize.
Can I withdraw from NPS before 60?
Partial withdrawals up to 25% of your own contributions are allowed after 3 years (not 10) for specific reasons — medical treatment, higher education, marriage of children, buying a house. You can do this a maximum of three times during the entire tenure. Full exit before 60 is allowed only after 10 years of membership, and then 80% of the corpus must go into an annuity with only 20% available as a lump sum. Exit before 10 years returns only your contributions (minus the returns) back to you. So NPS is genuinely a long-term product.
Is NPS better than PPF or EPF?
They're different instruments. PPF is guaranteed at 7.1% and fully tax-free (EEE), with a 15-year horizon. EPF earns 8.25% (FY 2023-24) and is also EEE for salaried employees. NPS is market-linked — 10–12% CAGR historically but with volatility, and it's EET (pension taxed). NPS gives a bigger corpus in good markets plus the extra ₹50,000 deduction, but the 40% forced annuity drags returns at retirement. Many planners suggest running NPS alongside EPF for diversification: EPF for the safety net, NPS for tax efficiency and equity exposure.
What is the minimum annuity I must buy at 60?
40% of your NPS corpus must be used to purchase an annuity from an IRDAI-registered life insurer (LIC, HDFC Life, SBI Life, ICICI Pru, etc.). You can choose to annuitize more than 40% — up to 100% if you want a larger lifetime pension. The annuity rate depends on the insurer and the product: a "life annuity" (pays till you die) gives around 6%, "life annuity with return of purchase price" gives around 5.5%, and "joint life with 100% to spouse" gives around 5%. The 60% outside annuity is a tax-free lump sum you can invest anywhere. There's no way to fully skip the 40% requirement.
Which NPS fund manager and scheme should I pick?
PFRDA-registered pension fund managers include SBI Pension Funds, HDFC Pension, UTI Retirement, ICICI Prudential, LIC, Kotak, Aditya Birla, Tata, and Max Life. Their long-term equity returns sit in a tight 11–13% CAGR band — differences are small. For allocation: Active Choice lets you set equity (E), corporate bond (C), and government securities (G) up to prescribed caps (75% E max under Tier-I, or 50% if you opt for the alternative). Auto Choice (LC50, LC25, LG) automatically reduces equity as you age. Most 25–40 year olds pick Active Choice with 75% E, 10% C, 15% G. Cost is low — total NPS charges are capped around 0.09% of AUM annually, one of the cheapest retirement products in India.
