Frequently asked questions

The seven questions people ask us most about percentage calculations.

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

To find X% of a number Y, divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by Y. For example, 15% of 250 = (15 ÷ 100) × 250 = 0.15 × 250 = 37.5. A quick mental shortcut: 10% of any number is the number with the decimal point moved one place left (10% of 250 = 25). From there, 5% is half of that (12.5), 20% is double the 10% value (50), and 25% is a quarter of the number. Our calculator handles the full formula instantly, no matter how awkward the numbers are.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

This is mode 2 in our calculator. The formula is: (X ÷ Y) × 100. For example, 45 is what percent of 150? Answer = (45 ÷ 150) × 100 = 0.3 × 100 = 30%. The rule of thumb is to always divide the smaller "part" number by the "whole" number, then multiply by 100. If you are computing test scores, sales conversions, or batting averages, this is the mode you will use most often.

How do I calculate a percentage increase or decrease?

For a percentage increase, subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100: ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100. Example: 200 going up to 250 = ((250 − 200) ÷ 200) × 100 = 25% increase. For a percentage decrease, the formula flips the direction: ((Old − New) ÷ Old) × 100. Example: 250 dropping to 200 = ((250 − 200) ÷ 250) × 100 = 20% decrease. Notice how the same two numbers give 25% up and 20% down — the base value always changes the percentage.

What is the difference between percentage and percentage point?

Percentage point measures the raw difference between two percentages, while percentage measures the relative change. For example, if interest rates go from 4% to 6%, that is a rise of 2 percentage points, but a 50% increase in relative terms. This distinction matters a lot in finance, statistics and news reporting — headlines often confuse the two. If you need the raw gap between two percentages, subtract them (percentage points). If you need the relative growth, use the percentage increase formula.

How do I add or subtract a percentage from a price?

To add a percentage (like GST or VAT), multiply the original price by (1 + percentage ÷ 100). Example: adding 18% GST to ₹1,000 = 1000 × 1.18 = ₹1,180. To subtract a percentage (like a discount), multiply by (1 − percentage ÷ 100). Example: 20% off a ₹500 item = 500 × 0.80 = ₹400. Shortcut for a 20% discount: multiply by 0.8; for 25% off, multiply by 0.75; for 10% off, multiply by 0.9. Our calculator handles both operations across its four modes.
