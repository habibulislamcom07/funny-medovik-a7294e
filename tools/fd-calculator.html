About the FD Calculator

A Fixed Deposit, commonly called FD, is one of the safest and most popular savings instruments offered by banks and non-banking financial companies. When you open an FD, you deposit a lump sum amount with a bank for a fixed tenure at a predetermined interest rate. The bank pays you interest on this deposit, and at maturity you receive your principal back along with the accumulated interest. Unlike market-linked investments, FD returns are guaranteed, making them ideal for risk-averse savers, retirees, and anyone looking for predictable growth on their idle money.

The interest earned on a fixed deposit depends on three key factors: the principal amount you deposit, the annual interest rate offered by the bank, and the tenure of the deposit. Longer tenures typically command higher rates, and senior citizens often receive an additional 0.25% to 0.50% interest. Most banks compound interest quarterly, which means the interest is calculated four times a year and added back to your principal — allowing subsequent interest to be earned on the larger balance. This compounding effect makes a meaningful difference to your final maturity amount, especially over long tenures.

The formula used by banks to calculate FD maturity is A = P(1 + r/n)nt, where A is the maturity amount, P is the principal deposit, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, n is the number of times interest compounds per year, and t is the tenure in years. For example, if you deposit $100,000 at 7.1% for 5 years with quarterly compounding, the maturity amount works out to approximately $142,300 — meaning you earn around $42,300 in pure interest. Compare this to simple interest, which would yield only $35,500 over the same period — a difference of nearly $7,000 thanks to compounding.

Our FD Calculator supports four common compounding frequencies used by banks worldwide — yearly, half-yearly, quarterly, and monthly. In India and many other countries, quarterly compounding is the standard for most bank fixed deposits, but some banks offer monthly or half-yearly compounding on select schemes. By switching between frequencies, you can instantly see how a small change in compounding schedule affects your total returns. This makes it easy to compare FD schemes across different banks and choose the one that offers the best effective yield for your money.

Fixed deposits also come with important considerations. Premature withdrawal usually attracts a penalty of 0.5% to 1% on the applicable interest rate. Interest income is fully taxable as per your income slab, and banks deduct TDS if annual interest exceeds the prescribed threshold. Despite these caveats, FDs remain a cornerstone of conservative financial planning because of their guaranteed returns, capital protection, and easy liquidity. They're perfect for parking emergency funds, saving for short-term goals, or balancing a portfolio that also includes equities and mutual funds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about fixed deposits.

What is a Fixed Deposit (FD)?
A Fixed Deposit is a financial instrument offered by banks and NBFCs where you deposit a lump sum amount for a fixed tenure at a predetermined interest rate. The bank guarantees the return of your principal along with interest at maturity, making FDs one of the safest investment options available.
How is FD interest calculated?
FD interest is calculated using the compound interest formula A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where P is the principal, r is the annual rate, n is the compounding frequency, and t is the tenure in years. Most banks in India compound interest quarterly, though some offer monthly or half-yearly options.
Does compounding frequency affect my FD returns?
Yes. The more frequently interest is compounded, the higher your maturity amount. For example, $100,000 at 7% for 5 years yields about $140,255 with yearly compounding, $141,478 with half-yearly, $141,478 with quarterly, and $141,478 with monthly. Quarterly is the standard for most bank FDs.
Can I withdraw my FD before maturity?
Yes, most banks allow premature withdrawal of FDs, but they charge a penalty of 0.5% to 1% on the applicable interest rate. This means you'll earn slightly less interest than originally promised. Some tax-saving FDs have a 5-year lock-in period and cannot be withdrawn early.
Is FD interest taxable?
Yes, interest earned on FDs is fully taxable as per your income tax slab. Banks also deduct TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) if annual interest exceeds the prescribed threshold — typically ₹40,000 for regular citizens and ₹50,000 for senior citizens in India. You can submit Form 15G or 15H to avoid TDS if your income is below the taxable limit.
Do senior citizens get higher FD rates?
Yes, most banks offer an additional 0.25% to 0.50% interest to senior citizens (typically aged 60 and above) on fixed deposits. Some banks also offer special senior citizen FD schemes with higher rates and flexible tenures. This extra interest can meaningfully boost retirement income.
