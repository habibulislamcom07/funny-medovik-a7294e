About the GST Calculator

The GST Calculator on Calcify is a free, browser-based tool that lets you add GST to a base price or remove GST from a gross amount instantly. Whether you are a shopkeeper preparing a bill, a freelancer creating a client invoice, a student learning the tax system, or a consumer trying to understand how much tax is buried inside a price you are being quoted, this calculator gives you a clear, accurate breakdown in a single click. It supports all four standard Indian GST slabs — 5%, 12%, 18% and 28% — along with special concessional rates of 0.25% and 3%, plus any custom rate you type in. It also handles the CGST + SGST split that Indian businesses use for intra-state transactions, and works with VAT-style taxes used in other countries.

GST (Goods and Services Tax) replaced a complex web of indirect taxes in India when it was introduced on 1st July 2017. Under the new system, most goods and services fall into one of the four rate slabs, and the tax is charged at every stage of the supply chain but credited back at each step. For a business, getting GST calculations right is essential — a single wrong invoice can lead to disputes with customers, penalties from the tax department, or overpayment of tax. For a consumer, understanding how much GST is hidden inside a price is useful for budgeting and for questioning whether a bill is accurate. Our calculator handles both sides of the equation with the exact same precision, and shows you the working so you can verify the numbers yourself.

The tool supports two distinct modes. In Add GST mode, you enter the base (net) price before tax, and the calculator shows you the GST amount and the final gross price the customer pays. This is the mode you use when quoting a price to a customer who is not registered for GST, or when preparing an invoice for a sale. In Remove GST mode, you enter the gross (inclusive) price the customer has paid, and the calculator extracts the base price and the GST component that was included. This is the mode you use when a customer gives you a total figure and asks "how much of this is GST?" — a common request from accountants, auditors and business owners reconciling their books.

Beyond the basic numbers, the GST Calculator also shows the CGST + SGST split when you enable it. In India, for sales that happen within the same state (intra-state transactions), the GST is split evenly between Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST) — for example, an 18% GST becomes 9% CGST + 9% SGST. For sales between different states (inter-state transactions), the full 18% is charged as Integrated GST (IGST) instead. Our calculator handles the intra-state case cleanly with one toggle, and the note below the split reminds you that IGST applies for inter-state transactions. Everything runs entirely in your browser, so your numbers are completely private, and the tool is free to use as often as you like with no signup and no limits.

Two Formulas — Both Handled Instantly

Add GST: Total = Base × (1 + Rate ÷ 100) — for when you have the pre-tax price and need the final bill amount.
Remove GST: Base = Gross ÷ (1 + Rate ÷ 100) — for when you have the inclusive price and need to extract the tax component.

How to use the GST Calculator

This is useful if you are outside India and using a VAT-style tax, or if you run a business across multiple currencies and want to see the tax calculation in the correct local format with the right comma style and symbol.

    The two tabs at the top of the form let you pick the direction of the calculation. Use Add GST when you have a base (pre-tax) price and want to know the final amount with GST added. The input label and hint text change automatically to remind you which number to enter.

  3. 3

    Enter the amount and pick the rate

    Type the price into the amount field. Then click one of the six rate chips — 0.25%, 3%, 5%, 12%, 18% or 28% — to apply the standard Indian GST slab instantly. If your rate is not in the list (for example, VAT at 20% in the UK, or a special concessional rate), just type it directly into the "Custom rate" field. The result updates live on every keystroke, so you can experiment with different slabs and see how the tax amount changes.

  4. 4

    Read the breakdown and enable CGST/SGST split

    The result panel shows the total price prominently, along with the base amount and the GST amount separately. If you are billing an Indian customer within the same state, tick the "Show CGST + SGST split" checkbox — a new panel appears that shows the GST evenly divided between Central GST and State GST (for example 9% + 9% on an 18% slab). The step-by-step working below the results shows exactly how the numbers were calculated, so you can verify or explain them to someone else.

  5. 5

    Copy the result and use it anywhere

    When you are happy with the calculation, click the "Copy result" button. The full breakdown — mode, amount, rate, base price, GST amount, total price and (if enabled) the CGST/SGST split — is copied to your clipboard in a clean, readable format. You can paste it into an email, an invoice, a chat message, a WhatsApp quote or a spreadsheet. Nothing you type is stored anywhere — the entire calculation happens inside your browser, so your prices and tax figures remain completely private.

Frequently asked questions

The seven questions people ask us most about GST calculations.

What is GST and how is it calculated?

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a single, unified indirect tax levied on the supply of goods and services. It was introduced in India on 1st July 2017 to replace a complex web of taxes like excise duty, VAT, service tax, CST and octroi. The basic formula is simple: GST Amount = Base Price × (Rate ÷ 100). For example, 18% GST on a ₹1,000 base price is ₹180, making the final bill amount ₹1,180. To reverse the calculation from a gross price (say, ₹1,180 at 18% rate), the base price is ₹1,180 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,000 and the GST is the remaining ₹180. All of this is handled automatically by our calculator.

What are the standard GST rate slabs in India?

India uses four primary GST slabs: 5% for essential goods and services like food grains, medicines and economy transport; 12% for processed foods and business-class air travel; 18% for most goods and services including electronics, restaurants and professional services; and 28% for luxury items like cars, tobacco and high-end appliances. In addition, special concessional rates of 0.25% (rough diamonds, rough precious stones) and 3% (gold, silver, jewellery) apply to specific categories. Some items like fresh vegetables, milk, curd and educational services are exempt (0% GST). The exact rate depends on the HSN (goods) or SAC (services) code of what you are selling.

What is the difference between CGST, SGST and IGST?

For sales within the same state (intra-state), the GST is split evenly between Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST). For example, an 18% intra-state GST becomes 9% CGST (collected by the Centre) and 9% SGST (collected by the State). For sales between different states (inter-state), the full GST rate is charged as Integrated GST (IGST), which the Central Government later distributes to the destination state. This means for inter-state supply at 18%, the invoice shows 18% IGST, not 9% + 9%. Our calculator shows the CGST + SGST split when you enable the toggle, and the note below reminds you to use IGST for inter-state supply.

How do I remove GST from an inclusive price?

Removing GST (also called reverse GST calculation) means finding the base price and the tax amount from a gross price that already includes GST. The formula is: Base Price = Gross Price ÷ (1 + Rate ÷ 100), and GST Amount = Gross Price − Base Price. For example, if a bill totals ₹2,360 at 18% GST, the base is 2360 ÷ 1.18 = ₹2,000, and the GST is ₹360. This reverse calculation is essential when a customer gives you only the final amount, when you receive an invoice that shows only the total, or when you are reconciling a bank statement with your sales records.

Is GST the same as VAT?

GST and VAT are conceptually similar — both are multi-stage destination-based indirect taxes collected on value addition — but GST is more comprehensive. VAT (Value Added Tax) was the predecessor system in India and is still used in many countries including the UK, EU member states, UAE and Saudi Arabia. The key difference: GST is a single tax applied on both goods and services across the entire supply chain, while VAT historically applied only to goods (with separate service tax) and had different rates at different stages. Practically, the math works the same way — Base × Rate ÷ 100 — so our calculator works perfectly for both GST and VAT calculations, no matter which country you are in.

Who needs to register for GST in India?

GST registration is mandatory in India for businesses with an annual turnover above ₹40 lakh for goods and ₹20 lakh for services (₹20 lakh and ₹10 lakh respectively in special category states). E-commerce sellers, inter-state suppliers, casual taxable persons and businesses already registered under older tax regimes must register regardless of turnover. Once registered, you must charge GST on all taxable supplies, issue GST invoices with your GSTIN, file periodic GST returns (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B), and deposit the collected tax with the government. However, our calculator is useful even if you are not registered — many consumers and small businesses use it to understand how much tax is inside a bill.

Is this GST calculator free and accurate for business use?

Yes to both. The calculator is completely free with no signup, no limits and no ads inside the tool itself. The GST math uses the standard formula accepted by accountants, chartered accountants and the GSTN portal, so the numbers are mathematically exact. However, for legal, tax-filing or audit purposes, always verify with a qualified chartered accountant and the official GST portal, because tax rules can change and specific categories (composite supply, reverse charge mechanism, exemptions, cess on luxury items) may affect the final tax liability. Treat our calculator as an accurate, fast reference tool that saves you time and gives you the numbers you can double-check against your accounting software.
