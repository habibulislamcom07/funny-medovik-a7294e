About the Unit Converter

Whether you are a student solving a physics problem, an engineer verifying a specification, a chef scaling a recipe from cups to grams, a traveller comparing kilometres to miles, or a developer converting bytes to gigabytes, this is the only converter you will ever need. Every conversion runs instantly inside your browser as you type — no submit button, no page reload, no waiting for a server response. The moment you change a number or select a new unit, the answer appears in the result panel with the full working shown below it.

Unit conversion is one of those everyday tasks that quietly trips people up. Do you multiply or divide when moving from inches to centimetres? Is a UK gallon the same as a US gallon? How many ounces are in a cup — and does that change for flour versus water? Does 100 Mbps equal 12.5 MB/s or 100 MB/s? Getting any of these wrong by a factor of 8 or 10 has real consequences: a recipe that fails, a project that misses spec, a purchase that does not fit. Calcify's Unit Converter uses internationally recognised conversion factors for every category, including the tricky distinctions between US and UK customary units, binary and decimal data storage units, and precise temperature formulas. You get the right answer, every time.

Beyond the primary conversion, the tool is genuinely educational. It shows you the formula being used, the calculation step by step, and a full reference table that displays your value expressed in every unit of the current category at once. This is perfect for students who want to understand the underlying relationship between units, for professionals who need to cross-check a value against multiple measurement systems at a glance, and for anyone who simply wants to build intuition about how units relate to each other. Common conversions are available as one-tap quick presets — for example "cm → inches", "kg → lbs" or "°C → °F" — so the most frequently used conversions are always a single click away.

The Unit Converter runs entirely inside your browser using plain JavaScript. No data is uploaded to any server, no signup is required, and there are no limits on how many conversions you can run. It works beautifully on phones, tablets and desktops, supports keyboard navigation and screen readers, and remembers your last used category so you can pick up exactly where you left off on your next visit. The interface is designed to be fast, clean and distraction-free — just you, your number, and the answer you need. Whether you are at your desk, in a lab, in a kitchen, or on the road, this tool delivers instant, accurate, private unit conversions — completely free, forever.

How to use the Unit Converter

  1. 1

    Pick the right category

    At the top of the calculator you will see horizontal tabs for each of the 15 categories — Length, Weight, Temperature, Area, Volume, Speed, Time, Data Storage, Pressure, Energy, Power, Angle, Fuel Efficiency, Frequency and Cooking. Each tab carries a small icon and a label so you can find the right one in a glance. Simply tap the category you need. On mobile, swipe the tab strip horizontally to reach the categories further to the right. The tabs are remembered between visits, so the next time you open the tool, the same category appears automatically.

  2. 2

    Enter your number

    Type the value you want to convert into the Value field. Decimal numbers are fully supported, so you can enter 12.5, 3.75 or 0.001 with complete precision. Negative numbers work too — they are especially useful for temperature conversions below zero, like −40 °C or −20 °F. The calculator reacts live as you type: there is no Enter key to press, no submit button to hunt for, and no waiting. If you change your mind, just edit the number in the field and the result refreshes instantly.

  3. 3

    Select your From and To units

    Use the two dropdown menus — From and To — to pick the units you are converting between. Both dropdowns list every available unit for the chosen category, and each option shows both the unit abbreviation and full name (for example, "Pound (lb)" or "Kilogram (kg)") so there is no ambiguity. If you want to reverse the conversion — say you started with metres to feet and now want feet to metres — just tap the round swap button between the two dropdowns and the values flip automatically, keeping your number intact.

  4. 4

    Read the result and formula

    Your converted value appears in the highlighted blue card, with the correct unit name shown right below it. Underneath, the "Conversion formula" panel shows exactly how the result was calculated — including the conversion factor used and the multiplication step. This is invaluable if you want to verify the result, teach someone how the conversion works, or simply understand the mathematical relationship between the two units. For temperature and fuel efficiency categories, the formula panel explains the special handling that those categories require.

  5. 5

    Compare across all units and copy the result

    A reference table below the formula lists your value converted into every unit in the current category at once. This is perfect for cross-checking — for example, seeing 100 cm alongside metres, millimetres, inches, feet and miles in a single glance. The source unit is highlighted so you always know which row is your input. When you find the value you need, tap "Copy result" and the full breakdown — category, input, and output — is copied to your clipboard, ready to paste into a document, spreadsheet, message or email.

Frequently asked questions

The seven questions people ask us most about unit conversion.

Which unit categories and units does this converter support?

Calcify's Unit Converter covers 15 major categories and more than 200 individual units. Length includes nanometre, micrometre, millimetre, centimetre, metre, kilometre, inch, foot, yard, mile, nautical mile and light year. Weight and Mass covers milligram, gram, kilogram, metric ton, ounce, pound, stone, US ton and UK ton. Temperature supports Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin with proper offset formulas. Area includes square millimetre through square kilometre plus square inch, square foot, square yard, acre, hectare and square mile. Volume covers millilitre, litre, cubic metre, cubic centimetre, fluid ounce (US and UK), cup (US and UK), pint (US and UK), quart, gallon (US and UK), tablespoon and teaspoon. Speed includes metre/second, kilometre/hour, mile/hour, knot, foot/second and Mach. Time spans millisecond through decade. Data Storage covers bit, byte, kilobyte through petabyte in both decimal (kB, MB, GB) and binary (KiB, MiB, GiB) variants. Pressure includes Pascal, kilopascal, bar, millibar, PSI, atmosphere and Torr. Energy covers joule, kilojoule, calorie, kilocalorie, watt-hour, kilowatt-hour and BTU. Power includes milliwatt, watt, kilowatt, megawatt, mechanical horsepower and metric horsepower. Angle covers degree, radian, gradian, arcminute, arcsecond and turn. Fuel Efficiency handles km/L, L/100 km, mpg (US) and mpg (UK) with inverse-unit logic. Frequency includes Hz, kHz, MHz, GHz and RPM. Finally, Cooking covers millilitre, cup, tablespoon, teaspoon, fluid ounce, pint, quart, gallon and gram variants for water, flour, sugar and rice.

How accurate are the conversion factors used?

Every conversion uses the exact internationally recognised standard factors — the same ones adopted by the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM), the US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), and the UK National Physical Laboratory. For example, 1 inch is defined as exactly 2.54 centimetres, 1 pound is exactly 0.45359237 kilograms, and 1 nautical mile is exactly 1,852 metres — no rounding is used for these primary definitions. Temperature uses the precise formulas with the correct 32°F offset for freezing point and the 273.15 K offset for absolute zero. The calculator displays results with up to 6–8 significant figures to balance precision with readability, and all arithmetic is done in double-precision floating point so you never lose accuracy on very large or very small values.

What is the difference between US and UK units, and which should I use?

Several units differ between the US customary system and the UK imperial system, even though they share the same names. A US gallon is 3.785411784 litres, while a UK gallon is 4.54609 litres — about 20% bigger. A US fluid ounce is 29.5735 ml, while a UK fluid ounce is 28.4131 ml — the US one is slightly bigger. A US pint is 473.176 ml, while a UK pint is 568.261 ml — the UK one is bigger. A US cup is 240 ml, while a metric cup used in many countries is 250 ml. Stone (14 pounds) is used for body weight in the UK and Ireland but rarely in the US. Our converter lets you pick the US or UK variant for gallons, pints, fluid ounces and fuel efficiency so you always know which system you are working in. If you are following a recipe from a specific country, match the country's system. If you are unsure, US units are the standard for most online recipes and Amazon product listings.

How do temperature conversions actually work between Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin?

Temperature scales use formulas rather than simple multiplication, because each scale has a different zero point. Celsius to Fahrenheit: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32. Fahrenheit to Celsius: °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9. Celsius to Kelvin: K = °C + 273.15. Kelvin to Celsius: °C = K − 273.15. Fahrenheit to Kelvin: K = (°F − 32) × 5/9 + 273.15. Kelvin to Fahrenheit: °F = (K − 273.15) × 9/5 + 32. Our calculator applies the correct formula automatically — you only need to select your input unit and target unit. It handles negative values like −40 °C (which happens to be exactly equal to −40 °F — the only point where the two scales meet) and extreme values like −273.15 °C (absolute zero, equal to 0 K) correctly. Kelvin never uses the degree symbol, and its values are always non-negative by definition.

What is the difference between decimal and binary data storage units?

Decimal units use powers of 1000: 1 kilobyte (kB) = 1,000 bytes, 1 megabyte (MB) = 1,000,000 bytes, 1 gigabyte (GB) = 1,000,000,000 bytes. Binary units use powers of 1024: 1 kibibyte (KiB) = 1,024 bytes, 1 mebibyte (MiB) = 1,048,576 bytes, 1 gibibyte (GiB) = 1,073,741,824 bytes. Hard drive and SSD manufacturers advertise storage in decimal units, while operating systems like Windows report storage in binary units. That is why a drive sold as "1 TB" shows up as about 931 GiB in Windows — a common source of confusion. macOS switched to decimal reporting in 2009 to match manufacturer conventions, so Finder shows 1 TB as 1 TB. Our converter lets you convert between both systems accurately, so you can see exactly how many binary gigabytes fit in a decimal terabyte, or how many decimal megabytes are in a binary mebibyte. This matters for storage planning, bandwidth calculations, cloud pricing comparisons and understanding why your "1 TB" drive does not show 1,000 GB in Windows.
