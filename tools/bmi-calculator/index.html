About the BMI Calculator

The BMI Calculator on Calcify is a free, browser-based tool that instantly computes your Body Mass Index — a simple screening measure that compares your weight to your height to estimate whether you fall into a healthy range. BMI was developed in the 19th century and has since been adopted by the World Health Organization as the standard first-line indicator of weight status for adults. Our calculator follows the official WHO formula: BMI = weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. If you prefer imperial units, you can switch to feet-and-inches and pounds with one tap, and the tool will automatically convert everything behind the scenes. Based on your result, the calculator classifies you into one of four WHO categories — underweight (below 18.5), normal weight (18.5 to 24.9), overweight (25.0 to 29.9) and obese (30 and above). It also shows you the healthy weight range for your specific height so you know exactly how much you should weigh to stay within the normal band. Everything is calculated inside your browser — your height and weight are never sent to a server, and you can run as many checks as you like without signing up, downloading an app or paying anything. The tool works beautifully on phones, tablets and desktops, and supports multiple languages.

How to use the BMI Calculator

  1. 1

    Choose your unit system

    At the top of the input panel, pick Metric (centimetres and kilograms) or Imperial (feet-inches and pounds). Most countries use metric, but if you are in the United States, the United Kingdom or a few other regions, imperial will feel more natural. Your choice is remembered for the next visit.

  2. 2

    Enter your height

    Type your height into the first field, or drag the slider to explore different values. In metric, use centimetres (for example 170 cm); in imperial, use feet and inches (for example 5 ft 7 in). Try to measure in the morning without shoes for the most accurate reading, since height tends to shrink slightly through the day.

  3. 3

    Enter your weight

    Move to the weight field and enter your current body weight. Metric users enter kilograms, imperial users enter pounds. Weigh yourself on a flat, hard surface with minimal clothing for the most consistent result, and try to weigh at the same time of day each time so you can track changes reliably.

  4. 4

    Read your BMI and category

    The result panel updates instantly the moment you change either value. Your BMI appears in the highlighted card, along with the WHO category you fall into. A colour-coded scale below shows exactly where you sit between underweight, normal, overweight and obese, and the healthy weight range box tells you the minimum and maximum weight that would keep you in the normal band.

  5. 5

    Track your progress over time

    BMI is a snapshot, not a verdict. Use the calculator once a month — or once a week if you are actively changing your diet or exercise routine — to see which direction you are heading. Small sustained changes of 0.5 to 1 BMI point over a few months are far healthier than dramatic crash diets. Hit “Copy result” to save the figures to your clipboard and log them in a notebook, spreadsheet or health app.

Frequently asked questions

The seven questions people ask us most about BMI.

What is BMI and how is it calculated?

BMI stands for Body Mass Index — a simple number that compares your weight to your height to estimate whether your weight is healthy for your size. The formula is BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². For imperial units, BMI = 703 × weight (lbs) ÷ height (in)². It was originally developed by Belgian statistician Adolphe Quetelet in the 1830s and is now the standard first-line screening tool used by doctors and health organisations worldwide, including the World Health Organization.

What is a healthy BMI range?

According to the World Health Organization, a healthy BMI for most adults falls between 18.5 and 24.9. A BMI below 18.5 is classified as underweight, 25.0 to 29.9 as overweight, and 30.0 or above as obese. These thresholds apply to adults aged 20 and older. Children and teenagers use different age- and sex-specific BMI percentile charts because their bodies are still growing, so an adult BMI calculator should not be used for them.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

BMI is a useful population-level screening tool but it has known limitations. It does not distinguish between muscle and fat, so athletes and bodybuilders often register as overweight despite low body fat. It also does not account for fat distribution, bone density, age, sex or ethnicity. For a fuller picture, doctors combine BMI with waist circumference, body fat percentage, blood pressure, cholesterol and other measurements. Use BMI as a starting point for conversation, not a final diagnosis.

Is this BMI calculator free and private?

Yes — completely free, with no signup, no ads on the calculator itself and no limit on how many times you use it. All calculations run inside your browser using plain JavaScript, which means your height and weight are never uploaded to any server and no personal information is collected. Nothing you enter is stored anywhere except your own device, so you can use the tool as often as you like with complete privacy.

Can I use this calculator for children or pregnant women?

This calculator is designed for adults aged 20 and older. For children and teenagers (ages 2 to 19), doctors use age- and sex-specific BMI percentile charts because body composition changes rapidly during growth — a BMI that is high for an adult may be completely normal for a growing teenager. Pregnant women should also avoid standard BMI interpretation because the extra weight of pregnancy, the placenta and amniotic fluid makes regular thresholds inaccurate. Speak to a paediatrician or your obstetrician for guidance in these cases.

What is the difference between BMI and body fat percentage?

BMI measures how heavy you are relative to your height, while body fat percentage measures how much of your weight is actually fat tissue. A person with a high muscle mass can have a high BMI but a low body fat percentage — think of a rugby player or a powerlifter. Conversely, someone with a “normal” BMI can still carry too much visceral fat around their organs. For a full picture of health, doctors use BMI, body fat percentage and waist circumference together rather than relying on any single number.

How can I move my BMI into the healthy range?

Sustainable change beats quick fixes. Aim for a modest calorie deficit if you need to lose weight (300 to 500 calories a day below your maintenance level), focus on whole foods like vegetables, lean protein and whole grains, and add at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity plus two strength sessions per week. If you need to gain weight, add nutrient-dense calories rather than empty ones. Track your BMI monthly, not daily, and consult a doctor or registered dietitian for a plan tailored to your health history.
