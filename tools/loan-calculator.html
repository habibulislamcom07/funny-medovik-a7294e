About the Loan Calculator

The Loan Calculator on Calcify is a free, browser-based tool that calculates the monthly payment, total interest and total repayment amount for any type of loan. Whether you are taking out a personal loan for a wedding, financing a new car, applying for a home mortgage, funding your education, or starting a small business, this calculator gives you the exact numbers you need before you sign any paperwork. It supports all major loan types — personal, car, home, student and business — each with its own typical interest rate range, and you can switch between them with a single click. The tool also supports eight currencies (USD, INR, EUR, GBP, AED, SAR, CAD and AUD) with proper locale formatting, so it feels native whether you are in New York, New Delhi, London, Dubai or Toronto. Every calculation runs instantly inside your browser — no signup, no data collection, no waiting.

The math behind loan repayment is straightforward but easy to get wrong. Every loan repayment uses what is called an amortization schedule, where each monthly payment is split between interest (calculated on the remaining balance) and principal (the amount that reduces your debt). Because interest is charged on the outstanding balance, the early months of a loan are mostly interest, and the later months are mostly principal. The exact formula used by banks worldwide is: M = P × [r × (1 + r)^n] ÷ [(1 + r)^n − 1], where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12) and n is the total number of monthly payments. Our calculator applies this formula with full floating-point precision, so the numbers you see here match what your bank will quote you at the branch. This is the same methodology used by financial institutions in every country.

Beyond the monthly payment, the Loan Calculator gives you the complete picture you need to make a smart borrowing decision. The total interest card shows exactly how much you will pay in interest over the life of the loan — often a shockingly large number that borrowers do not fully appreciate until they see it laid out. The donut chart visually shows the split between principal and interest as a percentage, making it clear why choosing the right loan term matters so much. For example, a $250,000 loan at 11% for 5 years costs about $76,000 in interest, while the same loan stretched over 10 years costs over $160,000 in interest — more than double. This calculator lets you experiment with different amounts, rates and terms instantly, so you can find the loan structure that fits both your monthly budget and your long-term financial goals.

Every calculation runs entirely inside your browser using plain JavaScript — your loan amounts, interest rates and personal financial data never leave your device. The tool works beautifully on phones, tablets and desktops, supports dark mode, four languages (English, Hindi, Spanish and Arabic) with right-to-left layout for Arabic, and is completely free to use with no signup and no limits. Whether you are an experienced borrower comparing offers from multiple banks or a first-time loan applicant trying to understand what you can afford, this is the cleanest, fastest and most private Loan Calculator you will find anywhere — and it will always be free.

The Loan Formula

M = P × [r × (1 + r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]
Where M is the monthly payment, P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the number of monthly payments.

How to use the Loan Calculator

  1. 1

    Choose currency and loan type

    First select your currency from the dropdown — USD, INR, EUR, GBP, AED, SAR, CAD or AUD. Then pick your loan type (Personal, Car, Home, Student or Business) from the quick-select chips. Each type automatically sets a typical interest rate for that loan category — for example, Home loans default to 6.5% and Personal loans to 11%. You can change the rate any time you want to match your bank's actual offer.

  2. 2

    Enter your loan amount

    Type the total amount you plan to borrow in the Loan Amount field, or drag the slider. The default of $250,000 works for a mortgage scenario; adjust it to match your actual requirement — whether you are borrowing $5,000 for a personal expense or $500,000 for a home. The slider supports amounts from $1,000 up to $10 million, covering everything from small personal loans to large business financing.

  3. 3

    Set the interest rate

    Move to the interest rate field and enter the annual rate quoted by your lender. Personal loans usually run 10–18%, car loans 7–12%, home loans 5–8%, student loans 4–7% and business loans 8–15%. The slider lets you explore rates from 0.1% to 30% so you can see how sensitive your monthly payment is to the rate. Even a 1% difference in rate over 5 years can cost thousands of dollars.

  4. 4

    Choose your loan term

    Enter how long you want to take to repay, and switch between years and months using the dropdown. Shorter terms mean higher monthly payments but dramatically less total interest; longer terms mean smaller monthly payments but a much bigger interest bill overall. Try adjusting from 3 years to 10 years and watch how the total interest card changes — that visual is the single best argument for paying off debt faster when you can afford it.

  5. 5

    Read the results and copy

    The result panel updates live with every change. The main card shows your monthly payment; the breakdown cards show the principal, total interest and total amount you will repay. The donut chart shows the principal-vs-interest split as a percentage. When you are happy with a scenario, hit "Copy result" to copy the full breakdown — monthly payment, principal, total interest, total payment and term — to your clipboard for pasting into a loan comparison spreadsheet, an email to your banker, or your personal financial notes.
