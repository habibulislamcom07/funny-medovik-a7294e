About the SIP Calculator

The SIP Calculator on Calcify is a free, browser-based tool that projects the future value of your Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) in mutual funds. A SIP is one of the most disciplined ways to build long-term wealth — instead of trying to time the market with a lump sum, you invest a fixed amount every month and let the power of compounding and rupee-cost averaging work in your favour over years and decades. Whether you are just starting your investment journey, planning for a specific financial goal like a house, education or retirement, or comparing different scenarios to decide on the right monthly amount, this calculator gives you a clear, honest projection in seconds. It shows you three numbers that matter most: the total amount you will actually invest, the estimated wealth gain from market returns, and the final maturity value at the end of your SIP period.

The calculation uses the standard future value of an annuity-due formula, which is the accepted industry method for SIP projections. Here is the formula in plain terms: FV = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i), where P is your monthly investment, i is the monthly rate of return (annual rate divided by 12), and n is the total number of monthly instalments. The multiplication by (1 + i) at the end accounts for the fact that SIP instalments are typically invested at the beginning of each month, not the end. This matches the convention used by AMFI, SEBI-registered advisors and most mutual fund platforms in India and abroad. Our calculator applies this formula with full floating-point precision, so the numbers you see here match the projections you would get from a professional financial planning tool.

Beyond the raw maturity figure, the tool gives you valuable context for making informed decisions. The "Invested" card shows exactly how much of your own money you will put in over the entire period, while the "Wealth Gain" card shows how much of the final corpus comes from market returns rather than your contributions. This is the single most powerful way to understand the true value of long-term investing — as your SIP duration increases, the ratio of wealth gain to invested amount grows dramatically, often crossing 50% after 10–12 years and exceeding 70% after 20 years. The donut chart makes this visually obvious at a glance. And because Calcify supports multiple currencies (INR, USD, EUR, GBP, AED, SAR and JPY), you can use the calculator wherever you are in the world without translating numbers in your head.

The SIP Calculator runs entirely inside your browser using plain JavaScript. No data is uploaded to any server, no signup is required, and there are no limits on how many scenarios you can run. Whether you are an experienced investor stress-testing a 20-year plan or a first-time saver wondering what ₹5,000 per month could grow to in 15 years, this tool gives you a fast, private and accurate answer — completely free, forever. Adjust the sliders, watch the numbers update live, and make confident decisions about your financial future.

The Power of Compounding

A ₹5,000/month SIP at 12% annual returns grows to ₹11.6 lakh in 10 years, ₹50 lakh in 20 years and ₹1.75 crore in 30 years. The longer you stay invested, the more dramatic the wealth gain becomes — because returns compound on top of previous returns.

How to use the SIP Calculator

  1. 1

    Choose your currency

    Select your preferred currency from the dropdown at the top of the input panel. Calcify supports seven major currencies — Indian Rupee (INR), US Dollar (USD), Euro (EUR), British Pound (GBP), UAE Dirham (AED), Saudi Riyal (SAR) and Japanese Yen (JPY). All input fields, sliders and results update instantly to use the correct symbol and comma style for your currency, so the projections feel native wherever you are.

  2. 2

    Enter your monthly SIP amount

    Type the amount you plan to invest every month, or drag the slider. Common starting points are ₹500–₹1,000 per month for beginners and ₹5,000–₹25,000 per month for more serious savers. As a rule of thumb, aim to invest at least 15–20% of your monthly income if possible — even a modest amount invested consistently over 20 years creates a surprisingly large corpus.

  3. 3

    Set the expected return rate

    Enter the average annual return you expect from your chosen mutual funds. Historically, well-diversified equity mutual funds in India have delivered around 11–13% per annum over long periods, debt funds 6–8%, and hybrid funds around 8–10%. For conservative estimates use 10% or 11%; for aggressive long-term equity-only portfolios you can go as high as 12–14%. Never assume more than 15% for long-term planning — unrealistic expectations lead to poor decisions.

  4. 4

    Choose your investment duration

    Enter how many years you plan to stay invested, using the field or slider. Shorter durations give you a quick snapshot, but the real magic of SIP happens between year 10 and year 30. Try adjusting the slider from 5 to 30 years and watch how the wealth gain grows non-linearly — that visual alone is the best argument for long-term investing. For retirement planning, use at least 20–25 years. For specific goals like a house down payment or your child's education, match the duration to your goal timeline.

  5. 5

    Read, compare and copy

    The result panel updates live every time you change a value. The highlighted card shows your final maturity value, while the cards below show the exact amount you invested, the estimated wealth gain, and the total return percentage. The donut chart visualises the split between your contributions and the market's contribution to your final corpus. Once you are happy with a scenario, use the "Copy result" button to save the entire projection to your clipboard and share it or save it for later. Nothing you enter is stored on any server — the entire calculation is private and stays on your device.

Frequently asked questions

The seven questions people ask us most about SIP investing and returns.

What is a SIP and how does it work?

A SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is a method of investing a fixed amount of money into mutual funds at regular intervals — usually monthly. Instead of investing a large lump sum at one time, you invest a smaller amount every month, which averages out the purchase cost across market ups and downs. This is called rupee-cost averaging. When markets are down, your fixed SIP amount buys more units; when markets are up, it buys fewer. Over the long term, this smooths your entry price, reduces the emotional stress of timing the market, and lets compounding work quietly in the background. SIPs can be started with as little as ₹500 per month and are fully automated once set up.

What return rate should I assume for SIP projections?

It depends on the type of fund. For long-term planning, most advisors recommend using conservative estimates rather than optimistic ones. Equity mutual funds in India have historically delivered around 11–13% per annum over 10+ year periods, but this is not guaranteed. Debt funds deliver 6–8% and hybrid funds 8–10%. For a diversified equity SIP, 10–12% is a realistic, prudent assumption. Never plan your goals around 15%+ returns, because if actual returns fall short, your financial plan will be underfunded. Our calculator lets you test multiple scenarios — try 10%, 11% and 12% for the same SIP amount and see how much the maturity value varies.

How accurate is this SIP calculator?

The calculator uses the standard future value of an annuity-due formula that is the industry norm for SIP projections — the same method used by AMFI, mutual fund platforms and certified financial planners. Given your inputs (monthly amount, expected return, duration), the mathematical output is exact to the rupee. However, the result is only as realistic as your assumptions: actual mutual fund returns vary year to year, and no calculator can predict future market performance. Treat the projection as a planning aid, not a guarantee. Real-life returns depend on the specific funds you choose, expense ratios, taxes on gains, and market conditions during your investment period.

Is SIP better than investing a lump sum?

Neither is universally better — it depends on your situation. Lump-sum investing tends to outperform SIP mathematically if you invest at the start of a long bull market, because your entire capital compounds from day one. But timing the market perfectly is impossible, and a lump sum invested right before a crash can be devastating. SIP is psychologically easier, safer against timing risk, and ideally suited for salaried investors who receive money monthly. Most financial advisors recommend SIP for salaried professionals building long-term wealth, and lumpsum only when you have a windfall (bonus, inheritance, property sale) and a long investment horizon. Many investors use both — a lump sum for capital and a SIP for ongoing monthly savings.

Are SIP returns taxable?

Yes. In India, gains from mutual fund SIPs are subject to capital gains tax when you redeem your units. For equity mutual funds held for more than 12 months, long-term capital gains (LTCG) above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year are taxed at 12.5% (as of 2024–25 rules). If held for less than 12 months, short-term capital gains are taxed at 20%. For debt funds, gains are taxed as per your income slab regardless of holding period. Each SIP instalment has its own holding period, which means your redeemed units are matched FIFO (first in, first out). Tax rules change, so please verify current rates with your tax advisor or the Income Tax Department before filing. Our calculator shows pre-tax projections — actual post-tax returns will be lower.

Can I stop or pause my SIP anytime?

Yes, and this is one of the biggest advantages of SIP over traditional recurring investments. You can pause, modify, or stop your SIP at any time through your mutual fund platform or AMC app — usually with just a few taps. There is no exit penalty or lock-in for regular open-ended equity or debt funds (though ELSS funds have a 3-year lock-in per instalment). If you pause or stop, the units you have already purchased continue to stay invested and grow; only future instalments are halted. This flexibility means you can adjust your SIP if your income changes, without losing what you have already built.

What is a step-up SIP and should I use it?

A step-up SIP (also called a top-up SIP) automatically increases your monthly investment amount by a fixed percentage every year — usually 5–10% to match inflation and salary increments. For example, if you start with ₹5,000/month and step up 10% annually, your SIP becomes ₹5,500 in year two, ₹6,050 in year three, and so on. Over a 20-year period, a step-up SIP can multiply your final corpus by 2–3x compared to a flat SIP, because your contributions grow alongside your income. It requires no extra discipline once set up, and many AMCs offer this as a simple checkbox. Our current calculator projects flat SIPs; multiply your final figure by 2.5–3x mentally if you plan to use a 10% step-up strategy.
