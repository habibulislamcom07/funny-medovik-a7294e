About the EMI Calculator

The EMI Calculator on Calcify is a free, browser-based tool that instantly computes the Equated Monthly Instalment for any loan — whether you are planning a home purchase, financing a car, taking a personal loan or setting up business capital. EMI is the fixed amount a borrower pays to the lender every month, and it combines two components: the principal repayment and the interest charged on the outstanding balance. Our calculator uses the standard reducing-balance formula that banks worldwide follow, so the numbers you see here match what your lender will quote you at the branch. Whether you are in India, the United States, Europe, the United Kingdom or the Middle East, you can switch between seven currencies — INR, USD, EUR, GBP, AED, SAR and JPY — and the calculator will automatically format results using the correct local comma style and symbol. Simply enter the loan amount, the annual interest rate and the repayment tenure, and the tool instantly shows your monthly EMI, the total interest you will pay over the life of the loan, and the total amount that will leave your pocket. A visual donut chart makes it immediately clear how much of your total outgo is principal versus interest. Everything is calculated inside your browser — no data is sent to any server, and you can use the tool as many times as you like without any signup or hidden charges.

    Type the total amount you plan to borrow in the Loan Amount field, or drag the slider to explore different figures. The slider range adapts to your selected currency so that it stays useful whether you are entering a small personal loan or a large mortgage.

  3. 3

    Set the annual interest rate

    Move to the second field and enter the yearly interest rate quoted by your lender — for example, 8.5% for a home loan in India or 6% for a mortgage in Europe. The slider lets you quickly explore rates from 0.1% to 30% to see how a slightly higher or lower rate changes your monthly outflow.

  4. 4

    Choose your tenure

    Enter how long you want to take to repay the loan, and switch between years and months using the dropdown. Shorter tenures mean higher monthly EMI but much lower total interest; longer tenures mean smaller monthly payments but a bigger interest bill overall. Try both to find the balance that suits your budget.

  5. 5

    Read, compare and copy

    The moment you change any value, the result panel updates live. You will see your monthly EMI in the highlighted card, along with the principal amount, total interest and total payment. The donut chart shows the principal-to-interest split visually. When you are happy with the numbers, hit “Copy result” to save the figures to your clipboard and paste them anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

The seven questions people ask us most about EMI calculations.

What exactly is EMI and how is it calculated?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed amount you pay your lender every month until the loan is fully repaid. It is calculated using the reducing-balance formula: EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / [(1 + r)^n − 1], where P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12) and n is the total number of monthly instalments. Each EMI contains both interest on the outstanding balance and a portion of the principal, with the interest component shrinking over time.

Which currencies does this calculator support?

Calcify supports seven currencies: Indian Rupee (₹), US Dollar ($), Euro (€), British Pound (£), UAE Dirham (د.إ), Saudi Riyal (﷼) and Japanese Yen (¥). Each currency uses its own number formatting — for example, ₹5,00,000 in Indian style versus $500,000 in US style — so the calculator feels native wherever you are. Switch currencies any time from the dropdown and all values, sliders and results update instantly.

Is this EMI calculator free to use?

Yes, completely free with no hidden charges, no signup and no limit on how many times you use it. The tool runs entirely inside your browser, so no data is sent to our servers and no personal information is collected. You can calculate as many loan scenarios as you want — compare 10-year versus 20-year tenures, adjust rates, test different amounts — all without ever paying anything.

Should I choose a shorter or longer tenure?

It depends on your cash flow. A shorter tenure means higher monthly EMI but significantly lower total interest — you pay off the loan faster and save a lot of money. A longer tenure reduces your monthly outgo, which helps if your budget is tight, but you end up paying much more in interest over the years. A useful rule of thumb is to keep your total EMIs under 40% of your net monthly income to stay financially comfortable.

Can I use this calculator for home, car and personal loans?

Yes — the formula is the same for all reducing-balance loans. Home loans usually run 15 to 30 years at lower rates (around 6 to 9%), car loans run 3 to 7 years at 8 to 12%, and personal loans are shorter with higher rates (10 to 18%). Just enter the appropriate amount, rate and tenure for your loan type, and the calculator will give you an accurate EMI for any of them, including education loans, gold loans and business loans.

What is the difference between flat rate and reducing balance EMI?

In a flat-rate loan, interest is calculated on the full principal for the entire tenure — so a 10% flat loan is much more expensive than a 10% reducing loan. In a reducing-balance loan (which almost all modern bank loans use), interest is charged only on the outstanding balance, which decreases every month as you repay. This calculator uses the reducing-balance method because it is the industry standard and gives you the true EMI you will actually pay.

Can I prepay my loan early to save interest?

Yes, and prepaying is one of the best ways to save on interest. When you make a part-prepayment, the amount goes straight towards the principal, which reduces your outstanding balance — and therefore the interest charged on it. You can either keep the same EMI and finish the loan sooner, or ask the lender to reduce your EMI while keeping the tenure unchanged. Check your loan agreement for any prepayment terms, since policies vary by lender and country.
